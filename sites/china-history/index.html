Chronological Scroll

Dynasties & Epochs

221 BCE – 206 BCE

Qin Dynasty

Qin Shi Huang unifies China under a centralized empire, standardizing script, currency, and weights while initiating construction on the Great Wall.

202 BCE – 220 CE

Han Dynasty

A golden age of economic prosperity, philosophical synthesis with Confucianism, and the initial opening of the transcontinental Silk Road.

618 CE – 907 CE

Tang Dynasty

Renowned as the apex of cosmopolitan culture, monumental poetry (Li Bai, Du Fu), foreign trade, and artistic refinement across East Asia.

960 CE – 1279 CE

Song Dynasty

Pioneered paper currency, gunpowder, typography, and naval compass tech alongside a flourishing urban landscape and landscape painting.

1368 CE – 1644 CE

Ming Dynasty

Constructed the monumental Forbidden City, launched Zheng He's maritime expeditions, and perfected fine porcelain production.

1644 CE – 1912 CE

Qing Dynasty

The final imperial power, expanding borders to their greatest extent before transitioning into the modern republican era of the 20th century.

Heritage & Artistry

Culture, Architecture & Legacy

Imperial Architecture

The Forbidden City

The political and ceremonial center of China for over 500 years. Built with strict Feng Shui principles, red walls, and yellow glazed tiles symbolizing supreme imperial authority.

Palace Craftsmanship

Hall of Supreme Harmony

The largest wooden structure in China. Here emperors presided over enthronements and royal weddings, demonstrating unparalleled mastery in traditional timber framing.

Sculptural Heritage

The Terracotta Army

Buried with Qin Shi Huang to protect him in the afterlife, thousands of life-sized terracotta figures stand in formation, each crafted with distinct, individualized features.

Military Craft

Guards of the Empire

An extraordinary archaeological marvel uncovering ancient infantry, archers, and cavalry units equipped with real bronze weaponry engineered to withstand millenniums.

Ceramic Art

Ming Blue-and-White Porcelain

World-renowned for delicate cobalt blue brushwork over pristine white kaolin clay. Highly prized as trade currency and luxury items across Europe and Asia.

Artistic Iconography

Imperial Motifs

From five-clawed dragons symbolizing royal sovereignty to lotus flowers representing purity, Chinese porcelain serves as a canvas for deep spiritual symbolism.

Territorial Reach Over Time

Qin Dynasty Sphere

The initial unification concentrated around the Yellow and Yangtze River valleys, connecting northern defense walls into a singular protective boundary.
