Chronicles of Dynasties

Walk through the rise and fall of imperial houses that forged China’s culture, philosophy, and boundaries across millennia.

Xia Dynasty

c. 2070 – 1600 BCE

The legendary first dynasty of traditional Chinese history, established by Yu the Great following his miraculous taming of the Yellow River floods.

Key Achievement
Transition from tribal chiefdoms to hereditary monarchy and bronze casting.

Shang Dynasty

c. 1600 – 1046 BCE

The earliest dynasty attested by archaeological evidence, famous for intricate bronze metallurgy and oracle bone divinations.

Key Achievement
Creation of oracle bone script, the direct ancestor of modern Chinese characters.

Zhou Dynasty

1046 – 256 BCE

The longest-lasting dynasty in Chinese history, introducing the concept of the "Mandate of Heaven" and giving birth to the golden age of Chinese philosophy.

Key Achievement
Philosophical foundations of Confucianism, Daoism, and Legalism.

Qin Dynasty

221 – 206 BCE

Founded by Qin Shi Huang, China's first Emperor who unified rival warring states into a centralized empire with standardized writing, currency, and weights.

Key Achievement
Construction of the first Great Wall and the creation of the Terracotta Army.

Han Dynasty

202 BCE – 220 CE

A golden age of economic prosperity and cultural expansion. The ethnic majority of modern China still calls themselves the "Han people."

Key Achievement
Opening of the Silk Road trade route and invention of papermaking.

Tang Dynasty

618 – 907 CE

Renowned as a cosmopolitan high point of Chinese civilization, celebrated for unprecedented flourishing of poetry, arts, and global trade.

Key Achievement
Golden Age of Chinese Poetry (Li Bai, Du Fu) and woodblock printing.

Song Dynasty

960 – 1279 CE

An era of extraordinary intellectual and technological advancement, commercial revolution, and landscape painting mastery.

Key Achievement
Invention of movable type printing, paper bank notes, and the magnetic compass.

Yuan Dynasty

1271 – 1368 CE

Established by Kublai Khan of the Mongol Empire, making Beijing its capital and welcoming foreign travelers like Marco Polo.

Key Achievement
Unification of Eurasian trade networks and dramatic flourishing of Chinese opera.

Ming Dynasty

1368 – 1644 CE

A period of intense restoration of native Han rule, massive naval expeditions led by Zheng He, and exquisite porcelain production.

Key Achievement
Construction of the Forbidden City and restoration of the Great Wall.

Qing Dynasty

1644 – 1912 CE

China's last imperial dynasty, established by the Manchu people, expanding China's territorial footprint to its modern vast shape.

Key Achievement
Compilation of the Siku Quanshu and territorial unification of modern frontiers.

Modern Era

1912 – Present

From the end of imperial rule to today's global powerhouse, transforming into an economic leader in technological innovation and high-speed infrastructure.

Key Achievement
Poverty alleviation, space exploration programs, and global economic leadership.

Innovation

The Four Great Inventions

Discover the four ancient discoveries of China that revolutionized global warfare, learning, navigation, and literature.

Paper Making

Perfected by Cai Lun during the Han Dynasty (c. 105 CE) using tree bark, hemp, and rags, replacing cumbersome bamboo strips and costly silk.

Printing Technique

Woodblock printing developed in Tang Dynasty, followed by Bi Sheng’s ceramic movable type printing in the Song Dynasty, accelerating global literacy.

Gunpowder

Accidentally discovered by Daoist alchemists seeking immortality elixirs in the 9th century, transforming warfare and celebration fireworks globally.

Magnetic Compass

First created using lodestone in the Han Dynasty for feng shui, and later perfected into magnetic needles during the Song Dynasty for maritime navigation.

Did You Know?

Amazing Historical Facts

Surprising milestones and curious secrets hidden within China's vast timeline.

01

World's Oldest Paper Money

During the Song Dynasty, Chinese merchants introduced "Jiaozi", the first official government paper currency, 500 years before Europe adopted paper money.

02

The Grand Canal Legend

Spanning over 1,700 kilometers, China's Grand Canal is the longest and oldest artificial river in the world, built to ship grain directly to northern imperial capitals.

03

Ice Cream Originated in China

Around 200 BCE, a frozen mixture of milk, rice, and snow was served in China, laying the early groundwork for modern frozen desserts.

04

The Great Wall Mortar Secret

Ming Dynasty builders mixed sticky rice flour into mortar, creating a durable compound that helped the stone walls withstand centuries of earthquakes and erosion.

05

The Giant Seismograph

In 132 CE, polymath Zhang Heng invented the first seismoscope—a bronze vessel that dropped a bronze ball into a toad's mouth to indicate the direction of an earthquake.

06

Ancient Tea Culture

Tea was originally discovered as a medicinal herb around 2737 BCE before evolving into the refined cultural ceremony enjoyed around the world today.
